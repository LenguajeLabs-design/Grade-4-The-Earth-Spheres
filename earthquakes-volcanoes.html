GEOSPHERE / EARTH IN MOTION

Earthquakes & Volcanoes

Why does the ground shake? How does new rock form?

Explore two ways the geosphere changes. Read the short passages, follow the models, and explain what happens.

Short passages · Visual models · Explain with evidence

01 / GEOSPHERE EXPLORER

Earth’s plates move

Earth’s hard outer layer is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates. These plates move very slowly.

Below the plates, the mantle is mostly solid rock. Over a very long time, this hot rock can flow slowly.

02 / GEOSPHERE EXPLORER

Why does the ground shake?

An earthquake is a sudden shaking of the ground. Many earthquakes happen when rocks move along a fault. A fault is a break in Earth’s crust where rocks can move.

The plates keep moving, but rocks along a fault can get stuck. When the rocks suddenly slip, they release energy. This energy travels in waves and makes the ground shake.

Follow the steps

  1. 1

    First, rocks get stuck.

  2. 2

    Next, rocks suddenly slip.

  3. 3

    Then, energy makes the ground shake.

03 / GEOSPHERE EXPLORER

How does a volcano erupt?

A volcano is an opening in Earth’s surface. Hot melted rock below the surface is called magma.

Magma can rise through cracks. When magma reaches the surface, it is called lava. Lava cools and becomes solid rock.

During an eruption, lava, gases, or ash come out of a volcano. Ash is made of tiny pieces of rock and volcanic glass. Some eruptions have slow lava flows. Others explode.

Simplified model · Not to scale

Magma: melted rock below the surface.

Lava: melted rock on the surface.

Ash and gases can rise into the air.

04 / GEOSPHERE EXPLORER

Compare and connect

Earthquakes and volcanoes both change the geosphere. Many happen near the edges of tectonic plates, but not all. An earthquake does not always cause a volcanic eruption.

Changes in the geosphere can affect other spheres. Volcanic gases and ash can enter the atmosphere. Lava can change a river’s path. These changes can affect living things in the biosphere.

Earthquake: rocks suddenly move, and the ground shakes.

Volcanic eruption: material comes out through an opening in Earth’s surface.

Think, point and explain

  1. Put the earthquake steps in order: the ground shakes; rocks get stuck; rocks suddenly slip.

  2. What is the difference between magma and lava? Use the model to explain.

  3. Choose one volcanic change. Explain how it can affect another sphere.
